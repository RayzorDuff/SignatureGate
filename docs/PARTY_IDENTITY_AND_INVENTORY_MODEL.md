# Party identity, capacity and access model

## Identities

An **individual** is a person. A **company** is a corporation, business or other organization entity. **Party** means either kind of identity.

Participant and Contributor are capacities held by a party; they are not people or independent identity kinds. An individual can be a Participant, a Contributor, both or neither. A company can be a Contributor, but cannot become a Participant merely because one of its individual contacts participates.

Keep identity history when a capacity ends. Enrolling a capacity does not create a second individual or imply that two parties with a shared contact value are the same identity.

## System access

Facilitator/Spiritual Practitioner, Document Reviewer, Donations Reviewer and Directory Manager characterize authorized system access. They are not additional people or party kinds.

Associate a sign-in account with an individual, then assign the intended appointments/permissions. An authorized reviewer or Facilitator/Spiritual Practitioner need not hold Participant or Contributor capacity. A Participant or Contributor does not gain system permissions merely by holding that capacity.

Configurable display labels do not grant access, merge identities or rewrite history. Existing implementation identifiers remain compatibility contracts until a reviewed change is accepted.

## Contacts, consent and notes

Contacts belong to identities, with reviewed purpose and provenance. Sharing a contact between capacities does not imply shared consent. Archiving a contact or ending capacity must preserve required history and avoid unintended changes to another party or purpose.

Administrative notes belong to the individual/company identity. Historical source notes remain preserved. A repeated migration must not duplicate old notes, reimport changed legacy text or restore a canonical note someone cleared. Authorized edits require a reason and atomic audit; failure must roll back the write.

Company contact people remain individual identities. Their participation or appointments do not grant those capacities or permissions to the company.

## Operational boundaries

Practitioner assignment and explicit grants determine scoped access. Access to a physical location does not imply inventory ownership or custody. Contribution review, operational deposit confirmation and external accounting completion are distinct responsibilities and states.

Participation and donation amounts do not automatically authorize a transfer. Agreement scope, valid evidence, participant eligibility and actor authority must be checked through the reviewed operational rules.

## Validation and privacy

Verify independent capacity enrollment/closure, nonparticipant reviewer/practitioner access, denied unassigned parties, reviewed contact ownership, independent consent, historical-note reapplication and audit failure. Record sanitized pass/fail and source/version provenance; source checks alone do not certify deployed behavior.

Keep actual identities, contacts, notes, financial values, account identifiers, product details and live configuration outside Git.
